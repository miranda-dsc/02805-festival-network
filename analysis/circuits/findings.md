We used the shared loader (Leeds, rebroadcasts, 2020 and 2021 excluded): 214 editions, 32,293 acts. Reach is the number of distinct festivals an act played in 2015-2025; 82% of acts played one. The null is a within-year curveball with 200 draws per year, keeping lineup sizes and act degrees (checked on the first and last draw). Same-brand pairs are excluded from the pair test.

1. National share (each festival counted once per act). 2 festivals: 0.79 observed vs 0.70 null (0.69 to 0.71), 3,144 acts; 3-4 festivals: 0.73 observed vs 0.66 null (0.65 to 0.67), 1,681 acts; 5+ festivals: 0.67 observed vs 0.61 null (0.61 to 0.62), 887 acts.
   This measures how much more acts stay in one country than random lineups of the same sizes would make them.
   It misses acts' home countries; counting festivals rather than editions keeps repeat visits to one festival from looking national.

   This measures which acts carry the country effect, with size defined from lineups only.
   It misses calendar and genre, which watts/ controls for, and deletions drop pairs without rerunning the null.

   This measures the national tilt of each class, which the absolute gaps in 2 hide: an act at k festivals makes k(k-1)/2 pair links, so wide acts dominate absolute counts.
   It misses calendar and genre, and the ratios outside GB rest on 57 pairs. By country outside GB (observed/null shared acts, 5+ festivals): BE 23/58.2, DE 3/2.5, ES 17/22.4, FR 9/2.8, PL 0/1.2. The low 5+ value outside GB comes mostly from Belgium, so we do not lean on it.
