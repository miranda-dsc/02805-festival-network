The panel includes festivals seen in at least 7 of the 9 remaining years (glastonbury, reading, rock for people, sziget, trees, victorious). Each year has 250 Curveball draws that preserve lineup sizes and act degrees. We fit the same festival-pair fixed effects to observed and shuffled Jaccard across calendar years.

1. Overlap changed by -0.034 percentage points per year within 15 panel pairs across 6 festivals and 104 pair-years.
   The panel null slope was -0.058, and the full set gave -0.033 versus -0.046 across 784 pairs and 44 festivals.
   Annual means exceeded the null in 9 of 9 panel years and 8 full years, and dropping one panel festival at a time gave excess slopes of +0.008 to +0.043.
   This measures changes in shared acts within the same festival pairs.
   It misses pairs observed only once and changes in act prominence.

2. Matching edition counts to 3 in the panel and 11 in the full set gave central 95% ranges for excess slopes of -0.044 to +0.109 and -0.056 to +0.068 percentage points per year.
   At 30 acts per lineup, observed slopes were -0.003 and -0.005, with excess slopes of +0.003 and +0.003, respectively.
   These checks measure sensitivity to the number of editions and acts observed.
   They miss changes in which acts Clashfinder captures.

3. From 2015 to 2025, the full set's share playing at least three festivals changed from 2.0% to 2.8%, the top 1% booking share from 3.1% to 4.1%, and Gini from 0.104 to 0.116.
   The panel endpoints were 0.1% to 0.2%, 2.0% to 2.3%, and 0.037 to 0.033, respectively.
   These measures describe how bookings are spread across acts.
   These descriptive values depend on edition coverage and miss which festivals shared the acts.

4. The median share returning from the previous observed edition was 7.3% across 170 comparisons.
   The null median averaged 1.2%, and one-year gaps gave 7.1% observed versus 1.4% expected.
   This measures the share of the current lineup made up of returning acts.
   It misses returns from editions outside our data.
