## Actionable, ranked

1. **The "exactly k" null is not matched to the cohort, so the 5+ result is probably understated.** The cohort is acts with k editions at k distinct festivals (no returns). The null reassigns their k editions at random, so the same festival can be drawn twice and is then collapsed (`country_share` with `distinct=True`), leaving the null with fewer distinct festivals than the observed act. The most-played-country share over fewer locations is higher on average, so the null mean is pushed up and the excess pushed down. The cohort is also selected: only 239 of 887 acts in 5+ never return to a festival, and acts that tour without returning are plausibly the more international ones. So "the exact-k check weakens it, especially for five or more" (+0.023 distinct, +0.005 edition) mixes a real effect with a null that is easier to beat by construction. Say that in the findings, or match the null (draw the k editions at distinct festivals only, for example by rejecting draws with repeats). Do not quote the 5+ exact-k number as "no country effect"; at 0.65354 against an upper band edge of 0.65334 it is on the edge, and that is all it shows.

2. **Outside GB, "57 pairs" is 57 pair-years, and the festival deletions do not test the real clustering.** They are 21 distinct festival pairs, and by country (2 festivals, observed / expected): Spain 37 / 2.7, Belgium 28 / 7.8, France 10 / 0.4, Germany 5 / 0.5. Deleting one festival at a time cannot remove a cluster. For 5+ the pooled 0.60 is Belgium: Belgium is 23 / 58.2, and the rest is 29 / 28.9, which is about 1.0. So "the broadest class is below chance outside GB" holds for Belgium only. Add a leave-one-country-out table (and report distinct festival pairs next to the pair-years). Until then, the two sentences in the gaps report, "two narrower classes are above chance outside GB across all festival deletions" and "broadest class below chance", should read as Spain and Belgium results.

3. **The conclusion is stated twice and the two do not agree.** The top of the gaps report and "Reading" say "the broad contrast survives", and the same paragraph says the exact-k check "weakens it". The table in the gaps report marks "Repeat booking loyalty" as "Checked, Done". Decide the sentence: with distinct festivals, the excess is +6.9, +7.6 and +5.3 points on all acts, and +4.4, +4.7 and +2.3 on the no-return cohort, so a country effect remains in the two narrower classes under both weightings, and the 5+ class is at best marginal on the cohort (subject to point 1). Mark the status "weakened for 5+", not "Done". There is also no uncertainty on the differences between classes; only a null band per class.

4. **Reach against tags and Wikipedia is a cross-tab, not a test.** It shows that narrow-reach acts are less often tagged and less often covered by foreign Wikipedia editions, which is expected, since an act at 5+ festivals is bigger. It does not say whether the country tilt of a reach class survives within a coverage stratum, which is the question that separates "small acts" from "untagged acts". Run the circuits tilt for tagged and untagged acts inside each reach class (or the reverse). Without that, the "Reading" paragraph rightly says it cannot identify which acts account for both contrasts; the paper should not claim a link between the two groupings.

5. **The check will go stale when Nandini reruns.** `run.py` keys on her labels (`"3-4 festivals"`, `"5+ festivals"` in `REACH`) and loads `circuits/run.py` by path. After the label fix to "3 to 4 festivals" (review_circuits point 6) or dropping the one-festival class, `REACH` will no longer match and `pairs_by_reach.csv` will not join. Rerun after her change, and key on `circuits.CLASSES` instead of copying the strings. Also say in the report which commit of `circuits/run.py` the numbers belong to.
