# References

## Festival booking, touring and lineup similarity

- **Priority.** Nick Crossley, Rachel Emms (2016). Mapping the musical universe: A blockmodel of UK music festivals, 2011 to 2013. *Methodological Innovations* 9, 2059799116630663. We cite this as the closest precedent for using shared acts to identify genre structure in UK festival lineups pooled across three years. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/2059799116630663)
- **Priority.** Carmen Lopez, Mark A. A. M. Leenders (2019). Building a local identity through sellout crowds: The impact of brand popularity, brand similarity, and brand diversity of music festivals. *Journal of Strategic Marketing* 27(5), 435 to 450. We cite this for a measure of same-season act reuse across Dutch festivals and its association with attendance. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/0965254X.2018.1430055)
- Patrick James Adler (2021). The Curating City: A Functional Account of the Agglomeration of Creative Industries. *University of California, Los Angeles*, PhD dissertation. We cite chapter 6 for evidence that US festivals repeatedly book a concentrated pool of performers and for an analysis of prior bookings and shared promoters. [Source](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2n95d1c4)
- Nick Crossley (2025). Music Worlds and Event Networks: An Exposition. *Cultural Sociology* 19(2), 159 to 180. We cite this for the distinction between links formed by shared performers and links formed by shared audiences. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/17499755231180182)
- Martijn Mulder, Erik Hitters, Paul Rutten (2021). The impact of festivalization on the Dutch live music action field: A thematic analysis. *Creative Industries Journal* 14(3), 245 to 268. We cite interviews with Dutch industry participants for the role of competition for headliners and the pressure to differentiate through programming. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/17510694.2020.1815396)
- R. Scott Hiller (2014). Exclusive Dealing and Its Effects: The Impact of Large Music Festivals on Local Music Venues. *Review of Industrial Organization* 45(2), 153 to 175. We cite this for evidence that festival exclusivity clauses can restrict local booking options. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11151-014-9428-x)
- Martijn Mulder, Erik Hitters (2025). Dancing on a tightrope: Uncertainty, risk perception and trust relationships in the live music industry. *European Journal of Cultural Studies* 28(4), 1075 to 1096. - Ola Johansson, Thomas L. Bell (2014). Touring circuits and the geography of rock music performance. *Popular Music and Society* 37(3), 313 to 337. We cite this study of North American tour itineraries to motivate geography as a constraint on where artists play. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/03007766.2013.798554)
- Pablo M. Gleiser, Leon Danon (2003). Community structure in jazz. *Advances in Complex Systems* 6(4), 565 to 573. We cite this as a precedent for projecting musician affiliations into a collaboration network, where a shared band gives the edge a different meaning from a shared festival bill. ## Does prior work answer our question?

Crossley and Emms already relate shared bookings to genre and discuss date clashes in their reasoning for pooling UK lineups over three years. Lopez and Leenders measure same-year act reuse within the Netherlands and use that measure to explain festival attendance. Adler studies repeat bookings and promoter affiliation in the US with the act as the unit of analysis. [Crossley and Emms](https://doi.org/10.1177/2059799116630663) [Lopez and Leenders](https://doi.org/10.1080/0965254X.2018.1430055) [Adler](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2n95d1c4)

The Mannheim paper forecasts artist bookings for German festivals at yearly resolution, so its analysis cannot separate calendar proximity or compare within-country and cross-country festival pairs. A DOI and title search found the companion paper as the only verified publication using this deposit. [Dataset registration](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.7801/822) [Companion paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.akbc-1.4/)

We found no study that jointly compares tag-based genre similarity, calendar proximity and shared country as predictors of same-year festival-pair overlap against a null that fixes lineup sizes and act booking counts. Our overlap analysis can test those associations. Promoter ownership, exclusivity and artist routing remain possible explanations. A claim about lineup homogenisation over time would require measuring a trend in overlap or genre similarity. [Adler](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2n95d1c4) [Mulder and Hitters](https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494241289318) [Johansson and Bell](https://doi.org/10.1080/03007766.2013.798554)
